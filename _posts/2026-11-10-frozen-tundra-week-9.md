# 🏈 Scott Gets Away with It. I Finally Get a Damn Win.

**Scott is 9–0. Richard's six-game winning streak and three-week scoring reign are over. The Mighty Wieners won an actual football matchup. Somewhere, the people responsible for maintaining the natural order are checking the wiring.**

Last week the closest game was decided by 22.1. This week Rickey beat Richard by **1.9** and Scott escaped Paul by **2.5**. Paul had the second-highest score in the league and a winning tight end on his bench. Richard had four separate bench substitutions that could have saved him. Phil, fresh off leaving 49.4 usable points behind, submitted the week's only perfect lineup. We have gone from mass incompetence to selective competence, which somehow feels more insulting.

The text thread was light on formal smack talk and rich in evidence. Scott appealed last week's power rankings with **“Rigged. Bigly”** and **“Stop the steal 🤣”**. Then he won the week. Paul explained precisely how he could have beaten him. Richard checked Survivor out of openly admitted self-interest. This league does not need a heated argument when everyone is willing to enter a signed confession into the record.

## 📊 THE SCOREBOARD

Both men got what they needed, and the rest of us got a conversation about why Scott deserves even more credit.

Paul's lineup was good enough to beat **ten of eleven** possible opponents. Matthew Golden delivered **27.4**, Bijan Robinson 23.7, Bo Nix 17.1, Rhamondre Stevenson 14.3 and Tetairoa McMillan 13.6. The Bears added 14.0. That is a real, functioning offense. Unfortunately, Scott was the one opponent who required the deluxe package.

Paul supplied the autopsy himself: **“I hadn’t put Kittle back in yet after a bye and Andrews had the Thursday game. F me out of 5 pts.”** The receipt is **George Kittle 7.8 minus Mark Andrews 2.9 = 4.9**. Make that one switch and Rome wins **132.9–130.5**, by 2.4. Paul's rounded five-point complaint is mathematically sound. His timing is less impressive.

This was a failure to return Kittle *from* a bye, not starting somebody *on* one. The distinction matters to our disciplinary committee, which meets wherever the beer is. Paul now has **five straight losses** and the most excruciating kind of 3–6 record: this week's losing score was better than everybody else's winning score except the bastard he actually played.

Scott's legal ceiling was 148.0. Paul's was 141.9. Those alternate universes do not cancel each other out; each bench counterfactual holds the opponent's actual lineup fixed. In the universe ESPN pays attention to, Scott wins by 2.5, takes the weekly crown and remains undefeated. He has now won games by **0.3, 3.2 and 2.5**. Somebody check whether his rabbit's foot has a rabbit's foot.
