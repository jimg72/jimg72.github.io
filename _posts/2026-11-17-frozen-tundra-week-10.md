# Three Tenths and a Funeral

**Scott finally lost. Shawn finally exhaled. The rest of us finally got something worth staying up for.**

Last week I asked somebody to beat Scott before he needed a second keyboard for the exclamation points. I also suggested that Shawn, fresh off the league’s lowest score, facing undefeated Scott would be a wonderful opportunity for fantasy football to do something deeply stupid.

For once, this publication has manifested something other than evidence of my own bad lineup decisions.

**Shawn 92.6. Scott 92.3. Nine straight wins, gone by three tenths.** Not a demolition. Not even a particularly good fantasy performance. Just a Monday-night hostage situation that dragged one of the last plays into the fate of a perfect season. Scott has spent weeks surviving tiny margins. This time the decimal point came to collect.

Richard had requested “a final drive with a chance for some drama in a largely uninteresting matchup.” Be careful what you wish for. By 9:42, he had the “72 dolphins” with champagne ready but “not opening just yet.” Shawn, meanwhile, was discovering that Justin Herbert can score over nine points on the opening drive and still turn the rest of your evening into a cardiovascular event.

Scott’s response: “I appreciate your support Hilton. But I’ll still be first with a loss.” Technically correct. Spiritually, a man already asking the restaurant whether they validate parking at his own funeral.

Elsewhere, Jerry stole my high-score money, Hilton and Phil both cleared 140, Danny lost despite scoring like an actual functioning franchise, and Paul got evicted from two different places at once. The league scored **1,399.8 points**, up **106.9** from Week 9. Four teams cleared 140. Our headline attraction combined for 184.9.

Prestige television is not always good football.

## 📊 THE SCOREBOARD

### Shawn 92.6, Scott 92.3

**The undefeated season dies in the slowest possible way.**

Herbert finished with **16.5**, just enough to push Shawn past the **16.2** he needed to tie. That is the entire difference between Scott posting another row of exclamation points and the rest of the league getting a national holiday.

Shawn’s 9:43 p.m. review was perfect: “The frustration that is Justin Herbert…scores over 9 points on opening drive and still hasn’t clinched this thing.” By 10:15, he was celebrating: “McDaniel with an actual good play call!” Hilton’s contribution near the finish: “Nite nite.” Everybody understood the assignment.

This was not a one-man rescue. Drew Lock’s **18.8** led Shawn’s lineup, and the Rams defense supplied **13.0**. Jonathan Taylor had 11.3, KC Concepcion 10.2, and the rest collectively agreed not to make the evening easy. Brock Bowers’ 2.8 and Tank Dell’s 3.6 are how a simple assignment becomes a group project.

Scott got **23.6 from Josh Allen**, **19.9 from Tony Pollard**, and 12.9 from Jordan Love. But Chris Olave’s 4.5 and Germie Bernard’s 1.0 left the door open. Josh Downs had **13.3 on the bench**. Downs for Bernard adds 12.3 and changes the winner. We are not pretending everybody should see the future. We are absolutely printing the receipt.

Scott remains **9–1 and first overall**. Shawn is **5–5**, with a win nobody will remember was only the eighth-highest score of the week. Flags fly forever. So does a screenshot of 92.6–92.3.

### Jerry 164.8, Richard 102.0

**The Clan takes the weekly crown. Richard keeps the island lifeboat.**

Jerry went from **82.4 and a Survivor exit** last week to **164.8 and the league’s best score**. Exactly double. Apparently getting kicked off the island was less a death sentence than a performance-enhancing substance.

Joe Burrow delivered **34.8**, Chuba Hubbard **31.7**, Deebo Samuel **24.4**, Jared Goff **23.8**, and Bhayshul Tuten **18.8**. That is a lineup with multiple people doing their jobs, a concept several other franchises should investigate. Jerry also extracted **98.5% of his best legal lineup**, the best mark in the league this week.

Richard got **44.3 from Christian McCaffrey**, the top individual starter performance of the week, and **19 from Cam Little**. Those two accounted for 63.3 of his 102.0. The other seven combined for 38.7. McCaffrey carried this team like he had been told the rest of the roster was a tax dependent.

The bench provided more material than the starting lineup: Nico Collins **19.9**, Blake Corum **19.4**, Kaelon Black **13.6**. Richard knew what was coming: “Blame will be assigned by Jim’s agent in the write up. I know bc I have Nico on the bench with a 20 this week.”

Correct. Consider the blame assigned. Even his best legal 141.6 would not have beaten Jerry, so this was not a stolen win. It was simply an unnecessarily ugly loss with a beautiful island escape attached. Richard drops to **6–4**; Jerry joins him at **6–4**.

### Jim 162.3, Paul 87.0

**The Wieners stand tall, then finish second.**

For the second straight week, I have the pleasure of reporting that my team won without needing to write an appeal to the commissioner. **George Pickens scored 38.0**, **Brock Purdy 32.2**, Amon-Ra St. Brown **23.8**, Deshaun Watson **23.4**, and James Cook **21.7**. Five guys combined for 139.1. I would like to thank them for briefly making me look qualified.

My own chat assessment: “It’s like we found the blue pill this week.” Unfortunately, Richard supplied the more complete diagnosis when the Clan passed us: “the mighty weiners finished too early and allowed the clan to snatch high score away for the week.”

We lost the weekly crown by **2.5**. Aaron Jones had **12.0 on my bench** while Tyler Allgeier started for **1.4**. That 10.6-point upgrade would have delivered a 172.9 and high score. I am delighted to provide this newspaper with fresh material at my own expense. “Preston don’t miss shit,” I said in the chat. Apparently that includes the editor.

Paul’s **Cam Ward led with 20.8**, followed by Bijan Robinson’s 15.7 and Quinshon Judkins’ 14.6. Nobody else reached 11. The final **75.3-point margin** was the week’s largest. After last week’s brutal second-highest-score loss, this one at least spared Paul the argument that the schedule alone had screwed him.

I’m back to **5–5**, with two straight wins. Paul is **3–7**, six straight losses, and now out of Survivor too. Rome was not built in a day, but we are getting a pretty good demonstration of the demolition schedule.

### Hilton 152.8, Danny 120.7

**Danny brings a good score to a great-score fight. Again, no refunds.**

Hilton’s **Dak Prescott scored 37.0**, Cam Skattebo **25.7**, Parker Washington **24.7**, and Isaiah Likely **23.2**. Four players, 110.6 points. Kirk Cousins added 17.1, and Hilton cruised to his second straight win and a **4–6** record.

This team is fourth in season points at **1,185.8**, yet only has four wins. Not every losing record belongs to a bad team. Some belong to a good team that keeps opening its schedule and finding a crime scene.

Which brings us to Danny. **Ja’Marr Chase 28.6**, Matthew Stafford **21.6**, CeeDee Lamb **16.7**: 120.7 overall, fifth in the league, a **7–4 all-play week**. In the actual matchup? Another loss. Eight straight. **1–9**.

There was no magic bench correction to fix this one. Danny’s best legal lineup reaches 127.0, still 25.8 short. Hilton simply brought too much. The record is hideous, but roasting Danny as if he submitted 60 would be lazy journalism. We have standards. Occasionally.

### Phil 141.0, Rickey 111.2

**Phil ends one streak and extends another.**

Patrick Mahomes’ **36.8**, Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s **27.1**, and Jayden Daniels’ **23.6** drove Phil to 141.0 and his **third consecutive win**. He is **7–3**, matching Rickey’s record while ending Rickey’s five-game winning streak.

Rickey got a monster **40.7 from Jahmyr Gibbs** and **29.0 from Trevor Lawrence**. That is 69.7 from two spots, followed by 41.5 from the other seven. The Steelers defense contributed **minus one**, which is the fantasy equivalent of showing up late and stealing office supplies.

Rickey’s lineup was **98.1% efficient**, with just 2.1 recoverable points. This was not a management collapse. Phil was simply better.

Phil, however, has paperwork. **Emmett Johnson scored 32.1 on his bench**, while Jadarian Price started for 10.9. Phil volunteered: “I can’t blame him for the Emmet J bench though, that was all my bad management.” A refreshingly direct confession. Add Mack Hollins over Christian Watson and Phil’s best legal total climbs to **164.3**, just 0.5 behind Jerry’s actual high score. A good win with an even better game trapped in the waiting room.

### Ashley 91.1, Thomas 82.0

**Someone had to win. Ashley graciously accepted.**

Chase Brown’s **24.3** did the heavy lifting, with Alvin Kamara at **14.9** and Jacoby Brissett **14.5**. Ashley only beat two other teams on the week, but one was the team ESPN put across from him. That is a very efficient use of an ugly score.

Thomas got **22.0 from C.J. Stroud**, **17.4 from DJ Moore**, and **16.5 from Derrick Henry**. The other six starters combined for **26.1**. If three guys are doing all the work and six are mostly watching, that is either a fantasy lineup or a road construction crew.

Ashley had **19.0 recoverable bench points**, including Stefon Diggs’ 17.3 while A.J. Brown started for 3.7. He still won by 9.1. Thomas’ best legal 85.7 would not have caught Ashley’s actual score.

Ashley snaps a two-game skid to reach **4–6**. Thomas falls to **3–7**, with the week’s lowest total. The island does not claim him because it already did that in Week 6. You cannot evict a man who has already moved out.

## 🔮 WEEK 11: THE ISLAND GETS A HEAD-TO-HEAD FINAL

**Richard (6–4) vs. Scott (9–1): The main event.** The last two Survivor teams also play each other. Scott tries to prove the first loss was a speed bump; Richard tries to end a two-game slide and put the biggest possible name on the island’s last exit ticket. Please check your benches before calling for champagne.

**Hilton (4–6) vs. Rickey (7–3): The dangerous underdog.** Hilton is rolling off 152.8 and two wins. Rickey wants to restart his streak before the Blue Division becomes a traffic jam. The records look less competitive than the scoring does.

**Ashley (4–6) vs. Jerry (6–4): The ceiling test.** Jerry brings the weekly crown; Ashley brings a win and room to improve his lineup. Ashley needs the offense, not just the scheduling department, to show up.

**Phil (7–3) vs. Shawn (5–5): No hero discount.** Shawn’s reward for ending Scott’s perfect season is the league’s three-game winning streak. Phil should not mistake the 92.6 for permission to coast, but Shawn probably should not make that his weekly target either.

**Thomas (3–7) vs. Paul (3–7): Somebody gets a fourth win.** Barring a tie, one team leaves this meeting with actual good news. Paul wants the six-game skid over; Thomas wants to wash off an 82.0. This is not the matchup the network flexes into prime time.

**Danny (1–9) vs. Jim (5–5): The trap has my name on it.** Danny just scored 120.7 and lost. I just scored 162.3 and found a way to complain. If I act like a man on an eight-game losing streak cannot beat me, this newsletter will be used as evidence next Tuesday.
