# 🏈 The Wieners Go Limp. Scott Survives by Three-Tenths of a Prayer.

The undefeated table is down to one chair. **Thomas beat Jim 118.0–94.8**, ending the Mighty Wieners' perfect start while Kyle Monangai spent Sunday running through NFL defenders and watching Jim's fantasy season from the bench. Meanwhile, **Scott survived Rickey by 0.3**, a margin so thin he could paint over it with one coat. Scott's victory message was appropriately measured: “I win by .3 points. 4-0!!” Somewhere, Rickey is still staring at Tyler Shough's final total like the missing points might be under the couch.

**Paul won the week with 139.0**, shipped Hilton a **48.8-point beating**, and took sole possession of first in White. **Richard won his second straight**, despite keeping enough points on his bench to open a competing franchise. **Shawn finally won**, and did it with a perfect scoring lineup. Four weeks in, everybody has tasted victory except Hilton, whose team has apparently filed a motion to postpone football until further notice.

And now the NFL adds byes to the injury pile. **Week 5 is the first bye week**, with **Carolina and Kansas City off**. The guys who just carried several teams will be unavailable this weekend. Congratulations on surviving September. October would like to see your depth chart. ([NFL bye schedule](https://www.nfl.com/news/2026-nfl-schedule-release-every-team-bye-week))

## 📊 THE SCOREBOARD

### Bareback Trailer Park Boys — 118.0 · Mighty Wieners — 94.8

**Thomas Cashes the Check. Jim Leaves the Money in His Other Lineup.**

Thomas got **28.8 from Javonte Williams**, **24.5 from C.J. Stroud**, and **16.0 from Spencer Shrader**, his Wednesday waiver pickup. That is a useful transaction: acquire a kicker, put him in, collect actual points. Thomas also corrected last week's costly receiver decision by starting **Michael Wilson, who delivered 13.0**. DJ Moore's 2.2 and Dalton Kincaid's 1.2 were ugly, but the rest of the lineup covered the bill. A banker finally put the productive assets where they could earn something.

Jim's **Brock Purdy scored 19.9**, James Cook 15.3, and the Seahawks defense 12.0. Marcus Mariota's **5.3 came before an early knee injury**; Washington confirmed an MCL sprain with no exact return timetable. That belongs in the bad-luck column. Nobody gets to pretend a manager should have predicted an in-game injury. The bench, however, deserves its own hearing: **Monangai 27.5 versus Jeremiyah Love 6.0**, and **Deebo Samuel 15.5 versus Jordan Addison 7.1**. ([Mariota update](https://www.commanders.com/news/final-thoughts-more-updates-on-washington-s-injured-players))

The math matters. **Monangai alone gets Jim to 116.3—still a 1.7-point loss. Monangai plus Deebo gets him to 124.7 and a 6.7-point win**, without changing Mariota at all. Chicago fed Monangai **30 carries for 146 rushing yards and two touchdowns**. Jim fed him a folding chair. This wasn't necessarily an obvious call before kickoff, but it is a spectacularly unpleasant receipt afterward. Thomas ends his two-game skid at **2–2**; Jim falls to **3–1** and, as he confirmed in the chat, exits the Survivor pool as well. Two departures, one bench. ([Monangai's monster day](https://www.chicagobears.com/news/you-just-want-to-keep-feeding-him-kyle-monangai-powers-bears-offense-vs-jets-146-yards-2-tds))

### 149ers — 111.0 · Luther Vandross Slow Jamz — 110.7

**Scott Wins by a Swizzle Stick. Rickey Finds a Third Setting: Agony.**

After last week's first-place finish, Rickey Bobby discovered you can be neither first nor last and still have a miserable evening. **Kenneth Walker III's 30.4** kept Scott upright, with **Josh Allen 20.2**, Chris Olave 15.6, Jordan Love 14.3, and Brandon Aubrey 12.0 providing the support. Saquon Barkley's **1.5 came with a hamstring injury**, so the Tony Pollard bench score is hindsight, not proof of a foolish start. Scott finished eighth in scoring and still walked out **4–0**. The man could fall through the bar's front window and land in the VIP section.

Rickey got **18.4 from Sam LaPorta**, **17.1 from Shough**, and **15.7 from Jahmyr Gibbs**. He extracted **98.8% of his best possible lineup score** and lost anyway. The tiny difference was at receiver: **Khalil Shakir 10.7 on the bench versus Luther Burden III 9.4 starting**. That swap produces **112.0 and a 1.0-point win**. Burden had scored 16.3 last week while Shakir managed 1.1, so this was a defensible choice with a rotten ending. Replacing Bucky Irving with J.K. Dobbins would add only 0.1 and still lose. The misery requires the right receipt.

Scott is the last unbeaten team, but next week's RB room comes with blinking lights: **Walker is on bye; Barkley is week-to-week with his hamstring**. Pollard and Braelon Allen are available roster options, while Zach Charbonnet has already been ruled out for Week 5. Going undefeated apparently earns you a complimentary staffing crisis. ([Barkley report](https://www.nfl.com/news/eagles-rb-saquon-barkley-hamstring-considered-week-to-week-avoids-long-term-injury); [Charbonnet update](https://amp.nfl.com/news/nfl-news-roundup-latest-league-updates-from-monday-oct-5))

### When In Rome — 139.0 · LiquidRice;SolidScat — 90.2

**Paul Ships a Beating. Hilton Requests Signature Confirmation.**

**Tetairoa McMillan scored 38.2**, the week's best individual performance, after **14 catches, 192 yards, and two touchdowns** in Carolina's win over Detroit. Last week he scored 2.7. This week he added **35.5 more** and turned Hilton into the packing material. **Bijan Robinson supplied 27.2**, Quinshon Judkins 18.6, and George Kittle 15.0. Paul posted **143.9 and 139.0 in consecutive wins—282.9 over two weeks**. Rome has gone from rebuilding to charging admission. ([McMillan's career night](https://www.panthers.com/news/the-lights-aren-t-too-bright-for-tetairoa-mcmillan-as-he-resets-the-panthers-record-books-with-a-dominant-performance-steve-smith))

Hilton's **Dak Prescott and Jameis Winston combined for 39.9**. His two starting receivers combined for **7.7**. Cam Little kicked in **14.0**, almost twice the receiver contribution, which is a depressing way to discover your team has a strong foot and no hands. Even Hilton's best possible scoring lineup reaches only **96.4**. There is no bench conspiracy here. Paul still wins by 42.6. Hilton is **0–4**, last in weekly points for the second straight week, and his scoring sequence now reads **148.7 → 106.0 → 91.6 → 90.2**. That's a staircase somebody forgot to stop building.

Richard suggested in the chat that hosting the draft and figuring out the oven had distracted Hilton from assembling a team. At this point, the oven has a stronger case for postseason participation. Hilton does get the league's hardest schedule, but that doesn't make 90.2 edible. Paul moves to **3–1** and leads White; Hilton gets another week to explain why the defense rests while the offense apparently never got out of bed.

### Jed York Trailer Park MVP's — 128.2 · PuxxPhil Cornbread Cardmakers — 125.3

**Richard Reaches Mediocrity. Phil Misplaces AA Ron.**

**Drake Maye 28.1, Nico Collins 27.3, and Tee Higgins 21.2** supplied Richard's rescue crew. Christian McCaffrey and Jaylen Warren added a combined **28.6**, enough to survive Geno Smith's 8.5 and Travis Kelce's 2.5. Richard is **2–2** after opening 0–2, and described his new ambition perfectly: **“next step, mildly above average!!”** Two wins have turned the project manager's status report from “on fire” to “within shouting distance of acceptable.”

Phil deserves the injury credit he requested in the chat. Jayden Daniels and Breece Hall supplied no Week 4 points from the sideline, yet **Emanuel Wilson delivered 25.5**, **Kirk Cousins 24.2**, and **Puka Nacua finally returned to the scoresheet with 23.2**. Puka's entire first three weeks totaled 9.9; this one performance more than doubled that. Phil scored third-most in the league and lost by **2.9**. His own postgame diagnosis: “And all I had to do was play a little AA ron.” Correct—with one important seating instruction. **Rodgers's 24.6 over Mahomes's 17.9 gives Phil 132.0 and a 3.8-point win.** Rodgers over Cousins adds only 0.4 and still loses. Even the accounting joke needs the right column.

Richard's confession was equally honest: he'd benched an RB who scored 31. **Kyren Williams actually had 31.7**, the league's biggest bench score, **17.6 more than Warren**. Tucker Kraft's 13.5 also beat Kelce by 11.0. Richard's total available lineup ceiling was **168.5**, with an eligible superflex reshuffle adding Malik Nabers's 20.2 while moving Maye to QB. He left **40.3 recoverable points** behind and won anyway. Phil brought a calculator to a bar fight; Richard brought a bench he never bothered to unfold. The next decision also needs an injury check: **Higgins is day-to-day with an adductor issue**. ([Higgins update](https://amp.nfl.com/news/nfl-news-roundup-latest-league-updates-from-monday-oct-5))

### Brock of Ages — 120.9 · AJ Brown and the Out of Towners — 115.8

**Shawn Finally Gets a Chemical Reaction. Ashley Leaves the Active Ingredient on the Bench.**

Last week Shawn said he wasn't sure he wanted to read another reminder of how bad his team was. This week, sir, please pull up a stool. **Jonathan Taylor 22.2, Carnell Tate 18.0, Brock Bowers 17.6, and Ashton Jeanty 15.6** led a lineup in which **all seven offensive starters reached double figures**. Herbert, Waddle, and Kyler all chipped in. Shawn started the highest-scoring eligible combination on his roster: **120.9 out of 120.9, 100% efficiency**. The chemical engineer finally found the mixture that doesn't just produce smoke and an apology.

Ashley got **22.7 from Bryce Young**, **21.8 from Zay Flowers**, **19.9 from Lamar Jackson**, and **16.0 from Ka'imi Fairbairn**. But **Alvin Kamara's 20.3 sat behind Jacory Croskey-Merritt's 6.0**, and **Rashid Shaheed's 11.8 sat behind Davante Adams's 5.2**. Either substitution wins: Kamara produces **130.1**, Shaheed **122.4**. Last week's newsletter noted Ashley already had Kamara as an option with Etienne unavailable. This week the option produced 20.3, and Ashley treated it like the pharmaceutical sample nobody takes out of the drawer.

Shawn gets his first win and moves to **1–3**. Ashley falls to **2–2** after consecutive losses, and now his quarterback cushion is shrinking: **Lamar's ankle sprain leaves only an outside chance of playing Week 5**. Bryce is on bye. Michael Penix Jr. is already on Ashley's roster, but one available quarterback doesn't fill two jobs. The Out of Towners have taken the team name a little too literally. ([Lamar update](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-network-lamar-jackson-dealing-with-ankle-sprain-outside-chance-to-play-in-week-5))

### Egbuka's Clan — 122.3 · Oh Shoughs! — 90.9

**Danny Fires Pitts. The Rest of the Kitchen Still Can't Pass Inspection.**

**Joe Burrow scored 27.5**, **Chuba Hubbard 25.4**, **Jared Goff 22.0**, and **Will Reichard 19.0** for Jerry. Reichard was a Thursday addition who went straight into the lineup and supplied a serious contribution. Those four totaled **93.9**, enough to beat Danny on their own. Justin Jefferson remained on Jerry's bench with zero points, and Emeka Egbuka managed 2.3, but Jerry had enough working parts to keep the assembly line moving. **122.3 is his best score of the season**, and he's back to **2–2**.

Danny finally terminated the Pitts subscription. “Check my lineup smartass, he's been fired,” he told the chat, and the transaction log backs him up: **Wednesday's successful claim of Ollie Gordon II dropped Kyle Pitts**. Gordon scored **17.0 starting**, a useful response to losing Achane and a player several other owners tried to claim. The move deserves credit. Unfortunately, **CeeDee Lamb's 32.8** had to drag most of the remaining lineup through the parking lot. **David Montgomery, Ja'Marr Chase, Jake Ferguson, and the Broncos defense combined for 12.1**. Even Danny's best scoring lineup only reaches **99.6**, still 22.7 short. You can't fix the whole restaurant by hiring one competent busboy.

Chase's **4.2 came before a concussion**, and he is now in the concussion protocol; that injury is bad luck, not a start/sit failure. Pitts, meanwhile, found a new fantasy employer: Scott added him Saturday, and his **6.2 stayed on Scott's bench**. No game swung because of the move, so let's hold the genius certificate. But getting fired by a 1–3 team and hired by a 4–0 team is one hell of a reference check. ([Chase update](https://amp.nfl.com/news/nfl-news-roundup-latest-league-updates-from-monday-oct-5))

## 📈 HEATING UP / 📉 CIRCLING THE DRAIN

### 🔥 Heating Up

**Paul:** Two straight wins, **282.9 points over those games**, and the White lead at 3–1. His **27–17 all-play record** is tied for second-best. Losing McMillan to this week's bye is a test of the empire's depth, not a reason to pretend the empire hasn't arrived.

**Richard:** **124.9 and 128.2**, consecutive wins, and an 0–2 start repaired to 2–2. The season all-play mark is still **21–23**, so “mildly above average” remains a goal. His last two weeks are genuine improvement. The unused bench points also suggest the ceiling is considerably higher than his current parking space.

**Scott:** Four straight wins, league-leading **535.3 points**, and the best all-play record at **32–12**. His scoring has declined each week—166.7, 132.4, 125.2, 111.0—but the overall case for first is still strong. This week's luck helped. The first three weeks built the cushion.

**Shawn gets a parole hearing, not a parade.** His first win came with his best score and perfect lineup efficiency. That's progress worth recognizing; one good week hasn't erased the other three.

### 🧊 Circling the Drain

**Hilton:** Four losses, three straight scores below 107, and back-to-back league-low finishes. The 148.7 opener is now the fantasy equivalent of an old profile picture. Yes, the schedule has been brutal. No, that doesn't explain why the offense keeps serving smaller portions.

**Danny:** **135.8 → 101.3 → 90.9**, two straight losses, and the league's lowest season scoring total at **405.9**. Losing Achane was real bad luck, Gordon was a useful response, and Chase's concussion makes the repair job harder. The roster still needs more than a new tight-end complaint department.

**Ashley:** Two straight losses after starting 2–0. The scoring bounced back from 97.1 to 115.8, so this isn't a collapse in production. It is a costly combination of unused bench help and a Week 5 quarterback problem. The name-based jokes can wait; his actual roster has volunteered stronger material.
