# 🏈 FTFFL — Week 2 Newsletter

## Danny Escapes the Basement. Rickey Asks If the Couch Is Included.

Last week, we measured the basement for Danny. This week, he scored a league-high **135.8**, handed Richard a **36.9-point beating**, and left the keys under the mat for Rickey. The Slow Jamz answered a 151.4-point opener with **83.6**, a 67.8-point collapse. That's not a sophomore slump. That's somebody unplugging the jukebox because the bartender needs to hear the police scanner.

Scott, Ashley, and Jim are **2–0**. Hilton, Richard, and Shawn are **0–2**. Ashley has delivered the biggest beating in the league two weeks running. Scott has Josh Allen operating like a cheat code. Jim has two wins, the seventh-most season points, and an increasingly suspicious relationship with the scheduling department. Somebody check whether he's buying that guy wings.

Meanwhile, the NFL spent the weekend wrecking quarterback rooms. **Jaxson Dart, Jayden Daniels, and Caleb Williams** all left games hurt, directly hitting Jim, Phil, and Hilton. Those injuries deserve context, not hindsight accusations. There's plenty of actual football malpractice elsewhere to keep the bar occupied. Kyle Pitts has now scored **2.0 points in two weeks**, and Danny keeps ordering another round.

## 📊 THE SCOREBOARD

### Mighty Wieners — 111.9 · Luther Vandross Slow Jamz — 83.6

**Rickey's Slow Jam Becomes Hold Music**

Jim needed three men to do most of the work: **Amon-Ra St. Brown 30.7, Brock Purdy 29.6, and James Cook 20.4**. That's **80.7**, just 2.9 short of Rickey's entire team. Aaron Jones added 10.5, and the Wieners won by **28.3** despite getting **1.7 combined from Colston Loveland and Dart**. This was less a balanced lineup than three guys carrying a sectional while everyone else held the door.

Rickey's Tyler Shough scored **23.1** and Jahmyr Gibbs added **20.3**. The other seven starters combined for **40.2**. Trevor Lawrence's 7.7 and Cameron Dicker's 2.0 didn't help, while Dallas Goedert's 0.9 came with a knee injury that ended his day. Goedert has a reported **MCL sprain and is expected to miss time**; Sam LaPorta's 14.2 on Rickey's bench is both painful hindsight and an immediate replacement option. Even Rickey's best legal lineup reaches only **98.2**. The bartender can't fix this by changing the channel. ([Goedert report](https://www.inquirer.com/eagles/dallas-goedert-injury-titans-status-tight-end-depth-20260921.html))

The bigger issue for Jim is what comes next. Dart hurt his knee on the Giants' opening drive Monday; Tuesday reporting raised the possibility of **season-ending surgery**, still unconfirmed at this writing. Jim had already **dropped Tua Tagovailoa and added Deshaun Watson as a free agent on Friday**, before the weekend's quarterback injuries. Watson then scored **20.1**. With Jim **12th out of 12 in waiver priority**, having that replacement secured before the scramble is a major roster-management win. Starting Dart wasn't a foreseeable mistake; getting insurance before he went down was excellent timing. Jim bought the spare tire before the highway filled up with tow trucks. ([Dart update](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/york-giants-jaxson-dart-faces-185543586.html))

### PuxxPhil Cornbread Cardmakers — 125.9 · LiquidRice;SolidScat — 106.0

**Hilton Finally Starts the Bench Heroes. They Clock Out Early.**

Hilton heard last week's lecture. **D'Andre Swift and Christian Watson both entered the lineup**, then combined for **23.5**, down from their 61.6-point Week 1 bench showcase. That's fantasy football: miss the concert, buy tickets to the next show, discover the band is doing an acoustic set. Dak Prescott's **30.3** and Omarion Hampton's **17.5** kept Hilton respectable, but Phil won by **19.9**.

Phil got **38.0 from Jaxon Smith-Njigba and 30.8 from Patrick Mahomes**—**68.8 together**, more than half his score. JSN's three-touchdown performance in Seattle's 31–7 win included an **82-yard touchdown**; apparently Drew Lock's job description was “find the guy Arizona has misplaced.” Phil's other starting receiver, Brian Thomas Jr., scored 5.5. One receiver hosted the party; the other brought a bag of ice. Meanwhile, **Puka Nacua's zero leaves Phil's first-round investment at 9.9 points through two weeks**. That's a strong entry for worst first-round return so far. The rest of Phil's roster is covering a very expensive bar tab. ([Seahawks recap](https://www.seahawks.com/news/rapid-reactions-jaxon-smith-njigba-scores-three-touchdowns-in-seahawks-dominant-31-7-win-over-cardinals))

Both managers now have quarterback concerns. **Daniels dislocated his elbow**, with Washington turning to Marcus Mariota while specialists assess him. Phil at least has **Kirk Cousins, who scored 21.9**, already on the bench. **Williams is week-to-week with a hamstring injury**; Chicago hadn't ruled him out for Week 3 in its Monday update. Hilton's only benched QB was Shedeur Sanders, who scored zero. His best possible Week 2 lineup was only **113.7**, so this defeat wasn't another hidden win sitting on the bench. This week, the cupboard actually needed groceries. ([Commanders update](https://www.commanders.com/news/jayden-daniels-dan-quinn-commanders); [Bears update](https://www.chicagobears.com/news/caleb-williams-week-to-week-with-hamstring-injury))

### 149ers — 132.4 · Bareback Trailer Park Boys — 117.8

**Josh Allen Picks Up the Tab. Again.**

Scott stays unbeaten because **Josh Allen scored 41.7**, the week's best individual performance. Allen threw three touchdowns and ran for two in Buffalo's **41–31 victory over Detroit**. Kenneth Walker III supplied 20.8, Chris Olave 18.6, and Brandon Aubrey 17.0. Scott's kicker outscored his Saquon Barkley–Mike Evans combination **17.0–9.4**. Some teams have depth. Scott has a guy kicking receipts over the crossbar. ([Bills highlights](https://www.buffalobills.com/video/bills-vs-lions-highlights-josh-allen-scores-5-tds-in-historic-home-opener))

Thomas got **22.7 from DeVonta Smith, 19.3 from Jalen Hurts, 19.0 from Dalton Kincaid, and 18.2 from C.J. Stroud**. DJ Moore's **−0.1** was a brutal hole, but an injury contributed to that result: he left Buffalo's game with a shoulder issue. This isn't evidence Thomas should have foreseen an early exit. Dontayvion Wicks's 9.9 would have recovered **10.0**, still leaving Thomas **4.6 short**. Even his best legal lineup loses. ([Moore injury coverage](https://ca.sports.yahoo.com/news/dj-moore-injury-latest-bills-013347919.html))

The **14.6-point margin** hides a useful distinction: Thomas posted the week's fifth-best score and would have beaten seven opponents. He just drew Scott. Barkley's **2.5** also came in a game where he suffered a stinger and returned, so Scott has his own health watch despite the 2–0 start. Allen can cover a lot of bar tabs; eventually somebody else needs to reach for a wallet. ([Eagles injury report](https://www.ksl.com/article/51626381/eagles-rb-saquon-barkley-stinger-te-dallas-goedert-knee-hurt-vs-titans))

### AJ Brown and the Out of Towners — 135.1 · When In Rome — 90.6

**Rome Falls. Ashley Doesn't Bother Keeping the Souvenir Cup.**

Ashley won by **44.5**, following last week's 67.9-point demolition. **Davante Adams erupted for 35.5**, Bryce Young supplied 25.2, and Stefon Diggs added 19.2. With Zay Flowers scoring zero on the bench, Diggs's production mattered. More impressive: **135.1 was Ashley's highest possible legal total**. He got every available point into the lineup and missed the weekly scoring crown by just **0.7**. And **Young's 59.4 through two weeks makes him QB2 among the league's rostered quarterbacks**, behind Allen's 78.4 and ahead of Mahomes's 54.2. The guy this room probably leaves undrafted without superflex is now serving drinks from the top shelf. Ashley has heard the jokes about his name for years; at 2–0, he's finally making everyone spell it correctly on the apology card.

Paul's high scorer was Cam Ward at **20.8**, followed by Bo Nix at 16.2 and Garrett Wilson at 14.2. Bijan Robinson managed 9.6. Detroit's defense gave him zero. After last week's specialist-assisted escape, Rome discovered that you cannot build an empire entirely around Evan McPherson. His 12.0 was fine. The empire was made of wet cardboard.

Paul also left **23.5 recoverable points** unused: Denzel Boston over Rome Odunze, George Kittle over Mark Andrews, and Quinshon Judkins over Rhamondre Stevenson. Even that perfect lineup loses **135.1–114.1**, but **79.4% lineup efficiency** was the week's worst. Ashley cleaned his plate. Paul spilled his nachos and then asked who ordered the mess.

### Oh Shoughs! — 135.8 · Jed York Trailer Park MVP's — 98.9

**Danny Finds the Gas Pedal; Richard Remains a Speed Bump**

Danny went from **77.9 to 135.8**, a **57.9-point rebound** and the top score of Week 2. CeeDee Lamb delivered **31.3**, Matthew Stafford **29.3**, and Ja'Marr Chase **23.0**. Those three combined for **83.6**, exactly Rickey's entire score. We interrupt Richard's beating to bring you another statistic Rickey won't enjoy.

Richard got **20.6 apiece from Christian McCaffrey and Travis Kelce**, plus 15.7 from Kyren Williams. Malik Nabers's **0.6** hurt badly; he left the Rams game with a shoulder issue before returning. The Giants' quarterback injury also changes the outlook for Richard's receiver going forward. His best legal lineup only reaches **110.4**, so this wasn't a win lost in the settings menu. It was another team failing to reach 100. ([Giants injury coverage](https://amp.nfl.com/news/giants-john-harbaugh-jaxson-dart-knee-injury-in-the-neighborhood-of-sprained-mcl))

Danny still managed to leave **Jake Ferguson's 18.3** behind **Pitts's 2.0**, a **16.3-point difference**. Last week Pitts scored zero while Ferguson scored 1.6. That's **17.9 points across two weeks** left behind at the same position. Yes, Pitts had the slightly higher projection this week. No, that does not stop the guy at the end of the bar from yelling “ORDER SOMETHING ELSE.”

### Egbuka's Clan — 106.8 · Brock of Ages — 103.1

**Jerry Wins the Bar Fight by Finding the Exit First**

Jerry survived by **3.7**, the closest game of the week, behind **Jared Goff's 31.1** and Joe Burrow's 16.9. Chuba Hubbard and Bhayshul Tuten added **26.6 combined**. After last week's unused Hubbard points, Jerry put him in and fielded his **best possible legal lineup**. A 106.8-point masterpiece is still a 106.8-point masterpiece. Sometimes the artist is working with crayons.

Shawn had **Jonathan Taylor's 27.2, Jaylen Waddle's 17.8, and Tyler Warren's 11.4**, but Justin Herbert and Carson Wentz combined for only **17.2**. Wentz was already in for the unavailable Kyler Murray; the replacement didn't solve the scoring problem. **Murray's −0.1 in Week 1 plus Wentz's 6.9 in Week 2 gives Shawn 6.8 total superflex points—3.4 per week.** Murray's injury was bad luck, but the combined output is still ridiculous: Shawn has turned the league's premium scoring slot into a two-drink minimum with no drinks. KC Concepcion over Carnell Tate was worth just **one extra point**, taking Shawn to 104.1. That's still a loss. There was no secret winning lineup; there was just a slightly less disappointing one.

Jerry gets his first win with the seventh-highest score. Shawn goes to **0–2**, with **193.5 season points**, lowest in the league and **0.1 behind Richard**. That is an extraordinary amount of suffering to separate two teams by the fantasy equivalent of a napkin.

**Hilton: 0–2, now with Williams hurt.** He has the fourth-most season points, so the record exaggerates how bad the scoring has been. But **106.0** was only eighth this week, and Scott is next. The schedule has stopped knocking and started trying the handle.

## 🍀 LUCK INDEX

*“All-play” compares a team's score with the other eleven teams each week. Through two weeks, that's 22 comparisons. It helps separate scoring strength from who happened to be on the schedule.*

**Luckiest bastard: Jim / Mighty Wieners.** Jim is **2–0**, but has finished **sixth in scoring both weeks**, holds a **12–10 all-play record**, and ranks **seventh in season points**. His opponents have totaled **181.5**, second-fewest in the league. The wins count. So does the fact that neither required beating a top-half weekly score.

**Unluckiest bastard: Hilton / LiquidRice;SolidScat.** Hilton is **0–2 with the same 12–10 all-play record as Jim**. He's scored **254.7**, a full **28.1 more than Jim**, and faced **280.8**. That's **99.3 more opposition points** than the Wieners have seen. Same all-play record, opposite actual records: one guy's getting complimentary wings, the other's been handed the whole table's check.

There are limits to the defense. Hilton's Week 1 bench choices could have won that game; this week's 106.0 was below most teams. Bad luck and preventable pain can occupy the same barstool.

**Ashley has the easiest schedule so far: 168.5 points against.** But he also has the second-most points and a **17–5 all-play record**, making his wins much better supported by production. Thomas shares that 17–5 mark yet sits 1–1 while facing the hardest schedule. Two weeks is enough to notice the imbalance, not enough to declare the universe personally invested in screwing Thomas.
