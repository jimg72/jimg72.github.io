# Three for Richard and No Fun for Jim

**Richard has won the week three straight times. Scott is still fucking winning. I'm still fucking losing. Welcome to the newsletter I voluntarily signed up to write.**

This was supposed to be fun. A few jokes, some friendly humiliation, a table or two. Then the Wieners started 3–0, lost five straight, and turned my little creative outlet into a weekly performance review conducted by people who enjoy my suffering.

Richard dropped **151.6** for a third consecutive weekly high and a sixth straight matchup win. Has anybody ever won the week three times in a row? Maybe. We are not looking it up. Richard gets the celebration; the historical record can remain somebody else's unpaid internship.

Scott is **8–0**. Phil benched **49.4 recoverable points** and still beat me. Danny selected a perfect lineup and still lost by 28.0. Not one of the six losing teams could have won by rearranging its existing roster. This week's Bench Receipt is less a prosecution and more a coroner's report.

## THE SCOREBOARD

### Jed York Trailer Park MVP's 151.6 vs SolidScat Again 129.5

**Richard Wins the Week Again and Hilton Discovers That Solid Is Not Enough**

Richard's supposed bye-week vulnerability lasted about as long as our interest in researching his place in history. With McCaffrey, Nabers and Collins parked on the bench, **Tee Higgins delivered 32.5, Drake Maye 22.6, Jaylen Warren 20.8, and Geno Smith 20.0**. That is 95.9 from four players before the rest of the trailer park opened for business.

Jameson Williams added 15.0, Tucker Kraft 14.3, Kyren Williams 12.4, the Rams 8.0 and Cam Little 6.0. Richard left only **0.9** recoverable points behind. Last week we roasted his luxury bench accommodations. This week he apparently read the fucking receipt.

**151.6. Three weekly crowns in a row. Six consecutive wins.** The commissioner is now taking the trophy home so often we should ask whether the league still owns it.

Hilton scored **129.5**, fourth best in the league, and lost by **22.1**. D'Andre Swift supplied 26.9, Dak Prescott 20.1, Jonah Coleman 18.4, Caleb Williams 14.9 and Christian Watson 14.3. T.J. Hockenson added 13.8. This was a real offense, not last week's emergency health-inspection waiver.

Rashee Rice and DeMario Douglas could have improved the receiver pair, but Hilton's **142.0** legal ceiling still loses by **9.6**. SolidScat Again is an encouraging brand revision. Unfortunately, Richard brought an industrial-strength plunger. Hilton falls to **2–6** with the week's highest losing score.

### 149ers 133.0 vs AJ Brown and the Out of Towners 71.1

**Scott Remains Undefeated While Ashley Fails the Calendar Test**

Scott finally started Tony Pollard after last week's 35.0 bench explosion and received **27.2**. Josh Allen supplied 28.6 and Jordan Love 23.6. Those three alone totaled **79.4**, already enough to beat Ashley. The rest of the lineup was essentially a victory lap with snacks.

Jacksonville's defense added 18.0 and Brandon Aubrey 13.0. Omar Cooper Jr. scored zero and Kyle Pitts managed 1.6, but Scott still won by **61.9**, the biggest beating of the week. Braelon Allen's 22.3 could have replaced Kenneth Walker's 10.4. Scott left 11.9 behind because apparently perfection in the standings requires no such thing in the lineup.

Ashley had **four zero-point starters: Lamar Jackson, Zay Flowers, Alvin Kamara and Ka'imi Fairbairn**. But two of those zeros deserve their own disciplinary hearing: **Kamara was on bye with New Orleans. Fairbairn was on bye with Houston. Ashley started both.** The [official Week 8 bye list](https://www.nfl.com/news/2026-nfl-schedule-release-every-team-bye-week) was not classified intelligence.

This was not a bad matchup call, a surprise inactive or a player getting hurt on the first drive. These two had no game scheduled. Their ceiling was zero before Ashley opened the app. **Leaving TWO bye-week players in your starting lineup is totally fucking unacceptable in a professional fantasy league such as ours.** We demand elite preparation from a league that calls a team the Mighty Wieners. The bar is already on the floor, Ashley. You brought a shovel.

The other five starters scored all 71.1, led by Tampa Bay's defense at 18.0 and Trey McBride at 16.7. Chase Brown had 13.1, Bryce Young 12.0 and Stefon Diggs 11.3. Five people clocked in. Two weren't even scheduled to work. Apparently the manager took their bye week too.

Kenny Gainwell's 14.0 sat unused while Kamara enjoyed his scheduled week off. This wasn't even a problem that required shopping for a replacement running back. Ashley already owned one. It wouldn't have threatened Scott, but it would have saved his Survivor seat. More on that professional negligence below.

Scott is **8–0**, still first in points, and still making this hobby worse for everyone else. Ashley is **3–5**, back in the loss column and newly off the island.

### Egbuka's Clan 141.5 vs When In Rome 84.3

**Burrow Declares War and Rome Does Not Have the Budget**

**Joe Burrow scored 44.7**, the highest individual performance on any league roster this week. Bhayshul Tuten added 28.9 and Chuba Hubbard 21.4. That trio produced **95.0**, more than Paul's entire team. Rome was conquered before Jerry reached the tight-end column.

Mike Gesicki chipped in 15.6 and Justin Jefferson 11.8. DK Metcalf's 0.9 and Will Reichard's 1.0 barely registered amid the artillery. Jerry wins by **57.2**, reaches **5–3**, and has three straight victories. His 141.5 was only 10.1 behind Richard. A terrific week, interrupted solely by the commissioner continuing to be an asshole about sharing.

Paul got 19.3 from Bo Nix, 12.0 from Chase McLaughlin and 11.3 from Garrett Wilson. Bijan Robinson and Quinshon Judkins combined for 13.5. Mark Andrews supplied 2.9. The empire's kicker outscored every offensive starter except Nix.

**Cam Ward's 27.6 and Matthew Golden's 17.4** watched from the bench. Paul's best legal rearrangement reaches 114.6, still 26.9 short. Yes, the seating chart was bad. No, changing it wouldn't have stopped the invasion. Rome falls to **3–5**, four straight losses, and another week of discovering that historical grandeur does not count as fantasy production.

### PuxxPhil Cornbread Cardmakers 97.9 vs Mighty Wieners 73.5

**Phil Submits Two Thirds of a Lineup and It Is Plenty**

Phil started Kirk Cousins for **29.1**, Jaxon Smith-Njigba for 20.8 and Puka Nacua for 19.5. Those three totaled 69.4. I scored 73.5. The remaining six starters were welcome to participate, but apparently their attendance wasn't essential.

Emanuel Wilson contributed zero. Jadarian Price had 4.7, Hunter Henry 4.5, Jason Myers 3.0 and Kansas City's defense 1.0. Phil could have used **Emmett Johnson 22.8, Harold Fannin Jr. 19.4 and Patrick Mahomes 27.0** to reach **147.3**. Instead he started 97.9, used only **66.5%** of his legal ceiling, and won by 24.4.

If you ever feel unqualified for management, remember Phil ran the least efficient operation in Frozen Tundra and still comfortably defeated the guy writing its annual report.

James Cook led my team with 17.1. Deshaun Watson added 13.6, Jalen Coker 9.6, Amon-Ra St. Brown 9.1 and Colston Loveland 9.0. Jeremiyah Love's 2.3 and George Pickens's 5.8 didn't help. Aaron Jones for Love and Jordan Addison for Pickens add 7.0. That's it. **80.5** was the best possible legal answer, still 17.4 behind Phil.

Last week's excuse was the winning lineup on my bench. This week there wasn't one. I have achieved the rare fantasy milestone of making better lineup decisions and becoming substantially less competitive. **Five straight losses. 3–0 to 3–5.** The newsletter has lost some luster; fortunately, my friends remain committed to polishing the knife.

### Luther Vandross Slow Jamz 120.3 vs Oh Shoughs! 92.3

**Rickey Plays the Hits and Danny Cannot Request a Better Roster**

**Jahmyr Gibbs 23.8, Bucky Irving 22.0 and Trevor Lawrence 21.0** carried Rickey's fourth consecutive win. Pittsburgh's defense added 16.0 and Luther Burden III 15.1. Drake London's 1.3 was a skipped track on an otherwise perfectly serviceable album.

Courtland Sutton over London adds 6.0, but Rickey didn't need the encore. He wins by 28.0, reaches **6–2**, and remains second in season points at 993.9. The Slow Jamz have scored **493.4 across four straight victories**. This is no longer background music.

Danny got 25.6 from Ja'Marr Chase and 20.2 from Josh Jacobs. Matthew Stafford and CeeDee Lamb each supplied 9.3, Daniel Jones 9.0, and Matt Gay 10.0. Jake Ferguson posted another zero, with no productive bench tight end available to fix it.

**92.3 started. 92.3 best possible. 100.0% efficiency.** Danny did the lineup part correctly. The inventory department remains under federal investigation. Six straight losses, five straight scores below 100, and a 1–7 record. This week there is no bench lecture. Just a respectful moment of silence for a man who found every point and still lost by 28.

### Brock of Ages 97.3 vs Bareback Trailer Park Boys 63.2

**Shawn Gets Back to Even and Thomas Goes Underground**

**Brock Bowers 24.7 and Kyler Murray 20.5** provided 45.2 of Shawn's 97.3. Chris Boswell added 9.0, Ashton Jeanty 8.8 and Antonio Williams 7.9. Jonathan Taylor's 5.7 was disappointing, but the other side had misplaced most of an offense.

Shawn could have improved by 11.6 with Waddle, Herbert and Mike Washington in the legal mix. He didn't need to. A **34.1-point win** brings him to **4–4**, ending the one-week interruption after his earlier three-game winning run. Not a masterpiece. A perfectly acceptable withdrawal from the Bank of Thomas.

Michael Wilson scored **22.8** for Thomas, Derrick Henry 13.0 and Cincinnati's defense 9.0. Baker Mayfield contributed 1.4. Jordan Mason 2.0. Tre Tucker zero. The starting quarterback, superflex, second receiver and kicker combined for **4.4**. That is a parking-meter balance, not a foundation for winning football.

DJ Moore 15.6 and Wan'Dale Robinson 13.7 could have replaced Tucker and Mason, lifting Thomas to 90.5. Still 6.8 short. His **63.2** was the league low, his fourth straight loss, and his third straight score below 95. The trailer park boys are now applying for basement zoning.
